[C1] When the source makes an abstract claim, connect it to the most useful concrete evidence already provided: a named example, before-and-after quantity, date, action, incident, or condition. Explain why that detail matters instead of reciting it as decoration.
[C2] Preserve the scale and meaning of numbers. Give a short plain-language gloss where needed and supported. Keep percentages attached to their populations and time periods. Do not invent a baseline, compute an unsupported comparison, or add precision absent from the source.
[C3] Explain a described incident with its documented sequence and stakes. Do not add a room, time of day, dialogue, physical reaction, or mental state to make it vivid. If the source supplies only a broad assertion, retain its limits instead of manufacturing evidence.
[C4] Make advice actionable using the source's actual actions, constraints, and tradeoffs. In coach mode, an additional illustrative example is allowed only when clearly introduced as hypothetical and kept distinct from evidence. Use only the one or two scenario details needed to explain it. In conversational mode, prefer the source's own examples.
[C5] Reuse a memorable concrete anchor when it genuinely helps connect a later idea. Avoid trivia, repetitive drilling for absent detail, fake interview questions, and excessive callbacks. Specificity serves understanding, not theatricality.
