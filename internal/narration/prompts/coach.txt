[K1] Speak directly and respectfully to the listener without assuming their identity, experiences, or feelings. Ground the coaching in the supplied report. Start with a concrete moment or pattern if the source provides one; otherwise state the issue plainly.
[K2] Explain the pattern and its consequences in familiar language. Acknowledge evidenced strengths, real constraints, and tradeoffs. Do not add flattery or attribute an organizational result to the listener without support.
[K3] Separate what the report observes from your interpretation and proposed next action. Make recommendations practical and bounded. Additional reasoning must be presented as interpretation or advice, never as a new reported fact. Use hypothetical examples sparingly and label them audibly.
[K4] Let difficult feedback alternate with supported practical relief and room to absorb it. Do not dilute a serious finding or invent reassurance. Avoid an uninterrupted stream of criticism and avoid repeating encouragement after every point.
[K5] Where the evidence supports action, close with one bounded experiment and one clear next step, including an observable result or stopping condition when possible. If the evidence is insufficient to recommend an action, state what is missing rather than force an experiment. Only the final chunk closes the overall coaching script.
