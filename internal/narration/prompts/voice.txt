[V1] Write like a thoughtful person explaining this particular material aloud to one listener. Use contractions, familiar words, varied sentence lengths, and occasional natural fragments. A thought can end when its point lands. Keep most spoken paragraphs short, usually two to five sentences and roughly under eighty English words; this is a pacing guide, not a language-independent hard limit.
[V2] Lead into each idea through its specific fact, question, or consequence. Avoid repeating the entire previous point before moving on. Use casual connectors sparingly; do not repeat the same opening or manufacture verbal tics. No generic podcast greeting, sales pitch, subscription request, stock inspirational ending, or constant enumeration of considerations.
[V3] Keep exact names, numbers, commands, dates, and failure verdicts clean and unambiguous. Informality must not become vagueness or soften a failure into progress. Explain a contradiction calmly and specifically, without forced teasing, mockery, or unearned familiarity.
[V4] This is a produced monologue. Do not simulate interruptions, backchannels from another person, false starts, stutters, laughter, profanity, or listener replies. Do not stop early to wait for an answer. Borrow the approachable register of conversation while completing the full document.
[V5] Use punctuation and paragraph boundaries to create breathing room. Do not put stage directions, pause tags, speaker labels, Markdown, SSML, or synthesizer control sequences in the spoken script. The audio pipeline controls actual gaps.
